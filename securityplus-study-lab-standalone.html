TELEMETRY + FORENSICS

Summary, full capture, and file clues.

LIGHTWEIGHT SUMMARY

NetFlow

Summarizes network-flow metadata: who talked to whom, when, and how much. Fast to query and generally lighter to store.

Think: traffic summary.
DEEPER PACKET EVIDENCE

PCAP

Packet capture records packets (headers and any captured payload). It can offer deeper forensic detail but uses much more storage.

Think: recorded packets.
FILE CONTEXT

Metadata

Data about a file: dates, size, format, owner, permissions—or image camera model, timestamp, and possible GPS data.

Think: details about the file.
Important: A packet capture does not magically decrypt encrypted traffic. Plaintext credentials are a concern when the application protocol or connection is not properly encrypted. Your teacher's examples include FTP, Telnet, SMTP, SNMPv1, and LDAP without SSL; read SMTP in the unencrypted-configuration context.
SCANNING + INTELLIGENCE

Find weaknesses before they become incidents.

Credentialed vs. uncredentialed scans

UNcredentialed

Scanner does not authenticate to the host. It sees network-facing services and exposed information.

Outside view
Credentialed

Scanner authenticates and can inspect local patches, software, processes, and configuration in much more depth.

Inside view

Tools named in class: Nessus and OpenVAS. A false negative means a real vulnerability exists but the scanner misses it. A false positive means the scanner reports a weakness that is not actually present.

Other evidence sources

  • Nessus / OpenVAS: vulnerability-scanning tools named in your teacher’s slides.
  • Daily endpoint scans: help track patch-installation status and find systems that still need updates.
  • Automated reports: recurring machine-generated findings.
  • Dashboards: a visual summary of security status and activity.
  • CVE: Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures; standardized public identifiers for known vulnerabilities.
  • OSINT: intelligence gathered from publicly available sources.
  • Threat feeds: updated indicators (such as suspicious IPs/domains) that tools can use for investigation or blocking.
Aggregation vs. correlation: aggregation means collecting logs in one place. Correlation means analyzing related events together to find a pattern. Collect the puzzle pieces first; connect the story second.

Threat hunting vs. vulnerability scanning

Your teacher’s slide contrasts alert response with proactive hunting. For exam accuracy, keep the ideas distinct: threat hunting proactively searches for hidden attacker activity or threats; vulnerability scanning searches for weaknesses such as missing patches and unsafe settings. Nessus/OpenVAS are vulnerability scanners.

Router hardening — class question

Your teacher’s scenario asks which administration feature to disable. The expected answer in that slide is web-based administration. The explanation is to reduce exposed management surface and use secure management methods such as SSH or out-of-band console access, according to policy.

Misinformation vs. disinformation

MISINFORMATION

False information shared by mistake; no intent to deceive.

DISINFORMATION

False information created or spread on purpose to deceive.

Good security awareness looks like this

  • Notice risky, unexpected, or unusual behavior.
  • Use password-management habits and protect credentials.
  • Removable media and cables: do not connect unknown USB devices or accessories; follow workplace policy.
  • Insider threat: consider that a trusted person’s actions—malicious or accidental—can create risk.
  • Operational security: avoid exposing sensitive plans, credentials, or work details unnecessarily.
  • Hybrid/remote work: protect screens, accounts, networks, and conversations outside the office.
  • Report suspicious messages using the organization's process.
THE OFFICIAL OBJECTIVE ALSO EXPECTS

Recognize, train, report, repeat.

Recognize unusual behavior

Look for behavior that is risky, unexpected, or unintentional—for example, a surprising access request, an unusual payment change, or a sensitive file sent to the wrong place. Context matters; investigate rather than jumping to conclusions.

Run an awareness program

Organizations develop and execute awareness activities, encourage initial and recurring reporting, and monitor whether people are recognizing and reporting suspicious messages. Training should be refreshed for new risks and work arrangements.

THE SAFE RESPONSE PLAYBOOK

Pause. Verify. Report.

1Pause

Urgency, secrecy, money changes, unexpected files, or credential requests are reasons to stop and think.

2Verify

Use a trusted contact method already on file—not the phone number or link in the suspicious message.

3Report

Use the organization's approved reporting path. Don't forward a suspicious link to classmates or coworkers.

BEC defense: A wire-transfer request should follow a documented process, such as independent verification and required approvals. A familiar name or voice is not proof by itself.
